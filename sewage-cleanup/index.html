A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Towson Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Towson, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
