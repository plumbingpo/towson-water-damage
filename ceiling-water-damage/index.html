Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Towson

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

Warning Signs

  • Paint that bubbles, blisters or peels away from the ceiling surface
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Electricity Overhead

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

Older Ceiling Materials

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Damage on the Floor Above

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.
