Crawl Space Water Removal in Towson

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
How We Fix It

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rot You Cannot See

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Water That Is Not Clean

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.
