Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Towson: What to Know

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelySee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • An intake screen packed with mud and stones so the pump cannot draw water.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Finished Basement Walls

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Furnace and Water Heater

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
  • Ongoing seepage that requires us to run a temporary pump in the pit.
  • The depth of the water and the share of the basement floor under it.
  • Whether the basement is finished, with drywall, carpet and trim to remove.

What We Do

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.
