Attic Water Damage in Towson

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. In Towson, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Fix the Cause

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.
