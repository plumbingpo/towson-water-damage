Wet Carpet Drying in Towson

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Towson homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
