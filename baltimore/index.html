How Baltimore Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Baltimore homeowner.

Pipes and fittings top the list. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Going away for more than a few days? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Towson Restoration is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in Baltimore. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

Common Water Damage Issues in Baltimore

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. The five below are ones we see constantly in Baltimore, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: Nothing is more time-critical than a broken water line, which keeps pouring until the supply is cut. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: A dishwasher that leaks from its door or a refrigerator line that drips behind the unit calls for same-day attention. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. For Baltimore kitchen jobs, our crew can frequently save the cabinets by pushing dry air in at the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. We extract the water, dry the wood to a safe reading and tell you what we think the source is.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. We check for hidden dampness, set up containment and clear out the affected material for homeowners in Baltimore.
